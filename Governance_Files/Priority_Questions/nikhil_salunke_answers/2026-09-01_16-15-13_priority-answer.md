# Priority Question Answer

Question:
What baseline are Decline Curve and Spot Feed outputs validated against before downstream consumption?

Answer:
Neither is validated against a single external "truth" table — each is gated against a reproducible anchor computed on every run, plus a frozen historical sample for method changes.

Spot Feed — hard blocking gate. spot_feed_calc.py recomputes both documented price anchors (WTI and Henry Hub, on the first-of-month convention) on every run and refuses to write the artifact, exit 2, if either misses the documented value by more than 0.5%. Rationale is in METHOD.md: under the wrong averaging method WTI was only 0.14% off and would have shipped, while Henry Hub was 10.5% off — so both anchors must reproduce, not just the well-behaved one. Secondary checks: the D-6 freshness/lag gate against EIA published settlements, MAX_AGE_DAYS (21), and byte-identical marketing/data/prices.json vs owner/data/prices.json.

Decline Curve — two baselines:

Per-run: selftest.py round-trips known synthetic Arps curves (qi within 6%, b within 0.25, MdAPE < 3% on noiseless data) and asserts the cleaning/QC/grading rules; each fit also publishes recent-nRMSE grade A–E and MdAPE against its own history, so the curve is scored on the months it was fitted to.
Per-method-change: the audit's frozen 7,158-lease sample is the regression baseline. Every parameter change is backtested on it before adoption — F035 (linear vs log-space: 32% vs 44% MdAPE), F042 (terminal decline 6%: +1% bias, MdAPE 32%→31%).

Caveat worth flagging on the ticket: the Spot Feed anchor gate proves internal method consistency against sec_basis.py, not price correctness against a licensed market feed — that's still open as T002/T006 (no CME/ICE licence, 6-day-old EIA settlements shipping where the 2026-07-16 ruling specified ~30s cadence).
