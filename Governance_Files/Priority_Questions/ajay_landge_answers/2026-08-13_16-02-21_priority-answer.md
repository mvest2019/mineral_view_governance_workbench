# Priority Question Answer

Question:
What is the rollback trigger and plan if organic traffic drops after the noindex rollout?

Answer:
The rollback trigger is straightforward — if we see a noticeable drop in overall organic traffic, impressions, or keyword rankings within the first 14 to 21 days after the noindex rollout, we act immediately. Specifically, we're watching for a 10–15% or greater decline in organic impressions or clicks at the site level compared to the pre-rollout baseline, any high-value keywords losing position that were previously stable, or pages we didn't touch suddenly dropping in performance due to internal linking disruption caused by the noindexed pages.

As for the rollback plan itself, since noindex is a tag-level change and not a URL deletion, the reversal is clean and fast. We simply remove the noindex meta tag from the affected pages, request re-indexing through Search Console's URL Inspection tool, and monitor for Google to pick them back up — which typically takes a few days to a couple of weeks. We roll out noindex in small batches rather than all at once specifically for this reason. If something goes wrong with batch one, we catch it before batch two goes live, and only a limited number of pages need to be restored. Every batch has its own before-and-after metrics logged so we can pinpoint exactly which group of URLs caused the issue without guessing.
