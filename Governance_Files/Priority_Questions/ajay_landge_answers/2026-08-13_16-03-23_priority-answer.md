# Priority Question Answer

Question:
What impression or click threshold decides that a URL is safe to noindex?

Answer:
We follow a simple rule — if a page has pulled zero clicks and fewer than 10 impressions over a rolling 12-month period in Google Search Console, it qualifies as a noindex candidate. That tells us Google is barely surfacing it and users aren't engaging with it at all.

But the threshold alone doesn't make the decision. Before tagging anything noindex, we verify the page isn't serving any other purpose — like supporting internal linking, backing a content cluster, holding backlinks with authority, or serving a conversion function even without organic traffic. We also check whether the page is simply new and hasn't had enough time to gain traction versus genuinely being dead weight. If the page is thin, duplicate, or parameter-generated clutter sitting at zero value with no strategic reason to keep it, noindex is the right call. If there's any signal worth preserving, we improve the page instead of pulling it from the index.
