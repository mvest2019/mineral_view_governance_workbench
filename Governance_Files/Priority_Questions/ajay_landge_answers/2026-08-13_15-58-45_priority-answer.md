# Priority Question Answer

Question:
What impression or click threshold decides that a page is safe to `noindex` versus kept indexed?

Answer:
There's no single universal threshold, but as a general working rule, if a page has received zero or near-zero impressions and zero clicks over a rolling 12 to 16 month window in Google Search Console, it's a strong candidate for noindex. Specifically, we typically look at pages that fall below around 10 impressions and 0 clicks over the last 12 months — that signals Google is barely showing the page to anyone and users aren't engaging with it at all.

Before applying noindex though, we cross-check a few things: whether the page serves an internal navigation or conversion purpose even without organic traffic, whether it's a newer page that simply hasn't had time to gain traction yet, and whether it's cannibalizing or diluting a stronger page targeting the same keyword. If it's thin, duplicate, or parameter-driven clutter with no search value, noindex makes sense. But if it has backlinks, supports a content cluster, or has even moderate brand-query impressions, we keep it indexed and improve it instead. The goal is to protect crawl budget and index quality without accidentally pulling pages that still have strategic value.
