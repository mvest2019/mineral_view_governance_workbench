# Priority Question Answer

Question:
Which dataset is now canonical for the Midland Basin valuation, and who approved the reconciled methodology?

Answer:
First, a distinction the question needs. The Midland Basin figure is an aggregation of county appraisal-roll values — the restricted README states plainly that these are "county-appraised values (public appraisal records), not market valuations" and that "'account' = appraisal owner number, not a verified individual." It is not MVEstimate, which is the separate model producing asset value, projected royalty income and reserve life and which carries its own open $0/NaN defects (Q-AI-0143, Q-AI-0538, both OPEN). Nothing about MVEstimate was reconciled. What follows concerns the appraisal-roll aggregation only.

No dataset has been designated canonical, no one approved the methodology, and the two candidate datasets produce materially different answers. The method itself is sound — I re-derived every published figure from source today. Verified against Mongo MineralOwnersInfoDB, Postgres MviewDownload, the live site and GovernanceDB on 1 Sep 2026.

The figures reproduce exactly. Running the documented two-stage method against source:

Which dataset produced it, and how stale it is. MineralOwnersInfoDB.Mineral_Owners_Data — 8,375,677 documents, value field Apprised_Value (misspelled), filtered on county_upper across the locked 15 counties. It is one of nine owner collections, and it is the oldest-written of the active four:

The valuation was computed on 28 July against a collection last written 28 May, and the organisation has since rebuilt the 2025 roll, the 2024 roll and the Master. The canonical figure is not merely undated — it sits behind three subsequent data rebuilds.

It is also not the dataset customers see. Q-AI-0410 (Rohit, 17 Aug) records that client-facing dossiers show "the CAD appraised value from the county appraisal roll (Mineral_Owners_Data_2025)". The basin figure and the per-owner values shown to customers come from different collections, two million documents apart.

Three structural problems with the collection the valuation used:

No appraisal-year field at all. Every other collection is year-partitioned; this one carries no year, so the figure cannot be tied to a specific appraisal roll.
Its value field is the one already ruled non-canonical. Q-AI-0087 (Vaishnavi, 7 Aug): "Canonical = Appraised_Value; both change together — rename the master DB field (Apprised_Value→Appraised_Value) and update the app reads in lockstep." That rename has not reached Mineral_Owners_Data. The valuation runs on a deprecated field name.
It is the only one of the nine carrying county_upper — the field the locked county definition matches on. The methodology is welded to this collection by schema: the 15-county filter cannot be applied elsewhere without rebuilding the county-matching logic.

What switching to the 2025 collection would actually do — and why it cannot simply be designated. Running the identical method there:

The headline would move from "3,922 accounts hold 94.3%" to "5,548 accounts hold 95.9%" — 1,626 more accounts, a 41% increase on the list that drives BD targeting. And the 2025 total is only computable after excluding 5,173 rows in the 15 counties where both Appraised_Value and ownernumber are NaN — unattributable records. The current collection has zero such rows. This NaN is in the appraisal roll itself, not the MVEstimate model, and I can find no prior record of it.

One consistency exposure to separate from the basin question. Client dossiers show per-owner CAD values from Mineral_Owners_Data_2025; the basin figures come from Mineral_Owners_Data. A methodological caveat that belongs in the sign-off. The dedup key I used — ownernumber + county_upper + leasenumber + Interest_Type + Interest_Value + Apprised_Value — is my reconstruction of the README's phrase "remove exact-duplicate interest documents." The documentation does not state the key. My evidence that it is right is that it reproduces 124,918 accounts, 3,922 ≥$1M accounts, $91.67B, 2,188,262 raw rows and Midland's 26,776 owners simultaneously and exactly. That is agreement, not documentation — and pinning the key down is precisely what a data-owner sign-off should do.

Recommended disposition — a scoped remediation, not a one-line designation.

Do not simply designate the 2025 collection. It changes the ≥$1M list by 1,626 accounts and carries 5,173 NaN records. Resolve those first: establish whether they are recoverable or must be excluded, and record the decision.
Raise the appraisal-roll NaN as its own defect, distinct from the MVEstimate $0/NaN items — 5,173 rows in the Midland Basin counties alone, with NaN owner numbers.
Port county_upper (or an equivalent normalised county key) to whichever collection is designated, or the locked 15-county definition cannot be applied to it at all.
Complete the Q-AI-0087 rename so the valuation stops running on a field already ruled non-canonical.
Re-run the method on the designated collection and publish both figures side by side with their write vintages and account counts, so the change from 3,922 to whatever the new number is happens visibly rather than silently.
Reconcile or retire countyspecific_mineralownerdata — a per-county table 3.8× above the canonical figure for its largest county cannot coexist with it unmarked.
Get a named data-owner signature on the dedup key, county list, threshold and as-of year, closing D-2, Q-AI-0208 and this question together.

The finding underneath. This one differs from the others: the analysis holds up. Every published figure reproduced from source today, to the account and to the dollar. What is missing is the same thing missing in Q-AI-0001, Q-AI-0002 and Q-AI-0003 — an owner. A correct methodology, run on a collection frozen since 28 May, using a field already ruled non-canonical, welded to that collection by a schema quirk, contradicted 3.8× by a second source in another database, while the product shows customers a third — and no signature anywhere. The number is right. Nobody is accountable for keeping it right.
