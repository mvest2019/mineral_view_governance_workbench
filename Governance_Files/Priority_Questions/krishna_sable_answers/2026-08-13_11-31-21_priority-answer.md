# Priority Question Answer

Question:
Why is the Coverage owner-roll cluster being de-indexed when it was flagged as the growth bet?

Answer:
Both are true, and they aren't in conflict — they describe different things. "Growth bet" is about where we invest. "De-index" is about the addresses those pages currently sit on.

The growth-bet finding stands, unchanged. Owner rolls are the best programmatic asset we have: 87 pages, 241 clicks, 1.33% click-through — the highest of any programmatic set on the site — and already ranking page one for Houston, Freestone, Panola, Leon, Anderson and Karnes county terms. Demand for county-level mineral-owner data is proven, not hypothetical.

Nothing about the de-index contradicts that. These pages are not being retired. They are first in the rebuild queue — the part of the section we most want back, fastest.

Three reasons they can't simply be left alone:

The address is Texas-shaped. The pattern is /mineral-rolls-data/texas/{county}. It was built when Texas was the only state. A national structure cannot grow out of it, and the alternative — bolting other states onto a Texas-scoped prefix — is exactly the kind of accumulated compromise that produced the current mess.
They share a section with the problem. Coverage and Operators together are 982 pages earning 14.1% of clicks. Thin pages weigh on site-level quality signals, not just their own — and that weight is carried by the blog library too, which earns 70% of our clicks. Clearing them is partly a defensive move on behalf of the blogs.
The content is thinner than the rankings suggest. Verified live: the owner-roll page ships a title and shell in its server HTML, with the owner table arriving via a follow-up request and rendering client-side. It ranks because Google renders JavaScript — but that is a dependency, not a foundation, and it's worth fixing while we're rebuilding rather than carrying forward.
The cost is real and named rather than glossed: proven page-one rankings will be surrendered and have to be re-earned. That is the sharpest edge of the whole decision, and it's recorded as a point to watch — though it does not reorder the rollout, which still leads with the mid-value cohort so the machine is proven on pages we can afford to get wrong.

Short version for the workbench: we are not abandoning the growth bet — we are moving it onto an address that can carry it nationally. The pages come back better, on a structure that scales, and first in line.
