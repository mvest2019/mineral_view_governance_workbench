# Priority Question Answer

Question:
Who approves building the "near me" and county-plus-operators pages, and does our live data coverage actually support what those pages will claim?

Answer:
Approval sits with Ryan, as part of the coverage architecture sign-off. These pages aren't a separate decision — they are the shape the county pages take inside Master Spec §2, the section still open.

There are two aspects here, and they need separating

1. The live site. It has been live and evolving for over two years. It is not a prototype — it carries 450+ genuinely registered users across the US and Texas, and 50–100 of them have gone as far as submitting a phone number. That is real intent from real mineral owners, and it is the strongest evidence we have that the concept works.

But the coverage and operator sections on that site were designed when Texas was the whole picture. Every structural choice in them assumes a single state. That was the right call at the time; it is the constraint now.

2. The upgrade. Ryan, with Claude's help, has built a considerably improved version — his vision, with added thinking and refinements over the current site. Alongside that, we are expanding beyond Texas to additional states.

Those two things together are exactly why a thorough check is needed now. A Texas-shaped coverage section cannot simply be carried across into a multi-state site — the structure would have to be redone later, at greater cost, with the traffic risk of a second migration.

What has to be checked together, not separately

Three sections are interconnected and cannot be settled one at a time:

Data Coverage — the county and state pages
Operators — the directory and the company entity pages
The Store — how data is sold, and how coverage pages feed into it

If we intend to sell data, the connection between them is the commercial path: a coverage page attracts the visitor, the operator data proves the depth, the store converts. Design any one of them in isolation and the other two end up contradicting it.

What the SEO work is contributing

We are proposing an architecture built to two requirements at once:

Welcoming to new states — adding New Mexico or Oklahoma should be a data load into structures that already exist, never a rebuild. Each state added on the current shape multiplies the problem; each state added on the proposed shape costs almost nothing.
Friendly to search and to AI answer engines — traditional search optimisation (SEO), plus Generative Engine Optimization and Answer Engine Optimization (GEO/AEO), so the pages are not only ranked but cited when someone asks an AI assistant. That matters increasingly: we are already the most-cited site among the competitors we track, and the structural properties that earn citations are the same ones that earn rankings — clean addresses, one page per real thing, real content above any gate.

And we are gathering evidence rather than asserting, so the resulting structure is defensible for search, for the user, and for the development team — dev should receive one specification to build from, not a set of opinions to reconcile.

Does the live data support what these pages will claim? Yes — verified this morning

The county-plus-operators page already exists and is already populated. /oil-and-gas-companies/texas/karnes-county returns 111 operator records, each carrying:

Operator name and operator number
Producing counties
Last production date
Lease count
Oil and gas produced, current through May 2026

Anderson and Midland counties return the same structure. So the page type is not speculative — it is live, real and current.

What is missing is not data. It is structure and search targeting. These pages sit on the legacy Texas-only URL pattern and are not written against the "near me" intent that actually drives this traffic — which is why competitors capture it and we largely do not.

Three things to get right before building
"Near me" implies currency. Someone searching that expects present-tense activity. Our production data runs to May 2026, so the pages should state their as-of date plainly rather than imply live data.
Counties differ in depth. Karnes has 111 operators; a quiet county may have a handful. The graceful-degradation rule covers this — show what exists, never an empty section — but it must be applied deliberately. A thin county page at scale is the exact trap the competitor evidence warns about.
In short: the data supports the pages. The work is structural — correct URLs, "near me" targeting, a visible as-of date,  and the coverage/operator/store connection designed as one thing rather than three.
