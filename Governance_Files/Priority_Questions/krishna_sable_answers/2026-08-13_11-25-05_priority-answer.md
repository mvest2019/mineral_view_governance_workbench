# Priority Question Answer

Question:
What are the 301 targets at cutover if the replacement pages don't exist yet?

Answer:
There are none yet, by design — and writing them now would actively cause harm.

The sequence separates the two steps:

Now: apply noindex. The pages stay live and working for users; they leave search results. No redirect at this stage, because there is nowhere to send anyone.
At each wave's cutover: the replacement pages ship, and the 301s for that wave only ship in the same deployment. Targets are defined per wave, at the moment they exist.
Why not write them up front. The replacement addresses depend on §2 and §3, which are open. If we authored redirects against today's URLs, then those URLs themselves moved during migration, we'd create chains — old → current → new. Each hop dilutes what passes through, and chains are one of the specific failure modes the migration runbook exists to prevent.

What we can define now, and have: the mapping rule. The shape is settled even though the literal addresses aren't:

County owner rolls → the county's mineral-owners section in the new structure
County wells → the county's wells section
County production → the county's production section
Numbered operator pages → the single clean-slug page for that company (many-to-one consolidation)
The four section hubs → their national-structure equivalents
One rule matters more than the mapping: a 301 only goes to a genuine equivalent. Redirecting a page to something unrelated — or sweeping everything to the homepage — is treated as a soft 404, passes nothing, and is worse than a clean 410. Any page with no real successor gets a 410, not a courtesy redirect.

During the gap — noindex applied, replacement not yet built — there are no broken links and no 404s. The pages serve normally to anyone who has the URL or arrives from a link. Only search visibility pauses.
