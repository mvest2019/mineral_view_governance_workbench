# Priority Question Answer

Question:
Are the working local prototypes reachable by anyone outside the team, and do they run on real production data?

Answer:
Not reachable by anyone. All four prototypes run on localhost on Krishna's machine only — ports 8099, 8100, 8101 and 8200. They have never been deployed, have no public address, and the servers are stopped between working sessions. Nobody outside the machine can reach them, including us, unless a server is deliberately started.

Not production data. Verified in the generator code this morning:

Every prototype builds from a deterministic, seeded sample world (random.seed(20260724) and random.seed(20260804)), so regeneration is stable and reviewable.
The sample world is 6 states, 44 counties, 9 basins, roughly 48 operators, 47 owners and 219 leases — a demonstration scale, not a data set.
No database access exists in any prototype. I searched the whole prototype tree for database drivers and connection strings and found none. They cannot reach production even by accident.
Three details worth stating for completeness:

Some operator names in the sample are real company names, used so the pages look realistic to review. Their county footprints, volumes and ownership figures are generated, not real.
The :8200 clone is an offline copy of Ryan's own marketing prototype, so its content came from that already-live site. It loads some images, fonts and map tiles from public CDNs, but it is still served locally only.
The demo login on the prototype lease pages is a client-side mock for demonstrating gated-versus-open treatment — it is not real access control, and nothing behind it is real data. Stating that explicitly so it's never mistaken for a security boundary.
One separate fact that may be behind the question: Ryan's own prototypes at mineralview-marketing.vercel.app and mineralview-owner-demo.vercel.app are publicly reachable (only if they have URL) — that's how we were able to make the offline copy. Those are his builds rather than ours, and the hash-based addressing keeps them out of search results while they're in progress.
