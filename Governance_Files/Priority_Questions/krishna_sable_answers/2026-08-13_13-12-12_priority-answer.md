# Priority Question Answer

Question:
What is the deadline for Ryan's final URLs, and does the 1C.3 placeholder block the go/no-go gate?

Answer:
On the deadline: none is set yet. Agreeing one would help everyone plan, and we're happy to work to whatever date suits the team.

Does 1C.3 block the gate? Yes, in practice. The Phase 3 launch-readiness sign-off requires every 1:1 redirect authored and tested with none pointing at a dead page, and the feature-page map in §1C.3 can't be completed until the final URLs exist. Writing those redirects earlier would create old → current → new chains, which lose value at each hop — so the order genuinely matters rather than being a preference.

The wider point, offered collaboratively. There's a sequencing question we'd like to align on with Ryan, the development team and the data science team — not to slow anything down, but because it's much cheaper to settle now than later:

Are we upgrading the features, or rebuilding the site structure, or both?
Are we expanding to other states? If yes, over what horizon?
Do the Data Store, Data Coverage, Operator Hub and Operator Directory align with each other — one clear role each, no two competing for the same search intent?
Is the URL architecture blueprint created and approved — where "approved" means the requirements have been walked through carefully for serving all of Texas and the wider USA as we grow, not just signed off in passing?
Why we're raising it. If the team is building supporting or underlying work — data pipelines, components, portal features — that's genuinely useful and independent of this. The care is specifically about building indexable page structures before the addressing is settled. If pages get built on the assumption that other states can be slotted in later, or that traffic will follow automatically, we risk repeating something that has already cost us once: the earlier page sprawl left the site crawl-capped, and recovering from it is a large part of the work we're doing now.

Framed positively: getting the addresses right once is a long, compounding investment — every state added afterwards is a data load rather than a migration. Getting it wrong means paying for the same migration twice and losing organic ground in between. That's the value at stake, and it's why we'd like the blueprint understood and agreed before page-building starts.
