# Priority Question Answer

Question:
Which measurement source will prove a page is a real organic-traffic contributor before anything is gated?

Answer:
Google Search Console, Pages report, on a clean 90-day window — that is the source of truth for the specific question of whether one of our pages is earning organic traffic.

Why Search Console for this particular question:

It is first-party truth — clicks and impressions as Google actually recorded them, not an external estimate.
GA4 answers a different question — what happens after the click, and registrations. Valuable, but it doesn't tell you whether a page earns search visibility.
Three rules that make the Search Console read trustworthy:

The 90-day rule. Only the most recent 90–120 days are usable — older data is inflated by the removed ~1.1M owner pages and ~24k operator pages.
Pages report, not Queries. About 84% of queries are anonymised, so query data understates reality; page-level data does not.
Fragments collapsed to their parent. An in-page anchor is not a page — getting this wrong made blogs look like 361 pages instead of 97.
Proposed explicit rule for "real contributor":

Contributor: any clicks in the 90-day window, or meaningful impressions at an average position showing real demand.
Not a contributor: zero clicks and negligible impressions across the full window.
Small-segment safeguard: for any segment under ~30 clicks/week, confirm a decline against impressions and position before acting — a 30% swing there can be seven to nine clicks of noise.
Ahrefs — the broader instrument, and where it is genuinely stronger
Search Console has one narrow strength: it is the only place that reports what Google actually recorded for our own pages. For almost everything else in SEO, Ahrefs is the far more powerful tool, and it is the one that will drive the rebuild. Search Console can only ever tell us about pages we already have; Ahrefs tells us about the entire market.

What Ahrefs gives us that Search Console structurally cannot:

Keyword research at depth — search volume, keyword difficulty, cost-per-click, matching and related terms, search suggestions, volume by country and volume over time. This is the evidence layer the whole coverage and operator rebuild depends on — it decides which pages are justified before we build them.
Top organic ranking pages — for our site and for competitors. We can see exactly which pages earn traffic across the market, which is how ShaleXP was analysed and how we confirmed the county-page model works commercially.
Competitor intelligence — organic competitors, their keyword sets, their best pages, where they beat us and where there's an opening.
PPC and commercial value — cost-per-click data, paid pages. CPC is the best available proxy for how commercially valuable a query is, which is why it's part of the rollout prioritisation alongside impressions.
Site audit — broken pages, technical issues, crawl problems, page-level content analysis. This is how ongoing technical health gets monitored rather than discovered by accident.
Rank tracking — our positions on chosen terms over time, which is one of the migration safety-net signals.
Backlinks and authority — referring domains, anchors, broken backlinks, domain rating.
AI and answer-engine visibility (Brand Radar) — and this one matters increasingly. Ahrefs can show what people are actually asking AI assistants, with volumes, plus which brands and pages get mentioned and cited in AI answers, and share of voice against competitors. That is directly the measurement layer for §11 of the Master Spec and for the AI-citation baseline — and it means that work can be measured with real data rather than only manual sampling.

The clean division of labour:

Search Console — what our existing pages actually earned. Used to decide what to protect and what to retire.
Ahrefs — what the market wants, what competitors earn, what a new page could be worth, what people ask AI, and whether the site stays technically healthy. Used to decide what to build.
One practical note on combining them: third-party tools estimate traffic for sites they don't own, so those estimates won't match Search Console exactly — we saw that with our coverage pages. That's inherent to external estimation for any tool, and it's simply why we take our own measured performance from Search Console. It takes nothing away from Ahrefs, which is doing the harder job of measuring the whole market rather than a single property.
