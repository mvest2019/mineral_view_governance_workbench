Three things that should reduce the governance concern:

Nothing is gone. A Search Console removal is temporary — roughly 6 months — and all 11 pages still return HTTP 200 today. This is fully reversible right now.
It is a hide, not a delete. Making it permanent needs a separate deliberate step (returning 410), which is on the dev hand-off list and has not been done.
The protect list is explicit. The same baseline names blogs, glossary and the homepage as protected assets not to be disturbed during migration. The removals were scoped to pages outside the earning set, not a trim of the blog library.
Where the process could be tightened: the removals were submitted before this governance question was raised, so the reasoning existed in the baseline but wasn't circulated for sign-off first. If the standard is that any change touching the blog namespace gets explicit approval regardless of traffic, that's a reasonable rule — and the reversibility above means it can be applied retroactively here.
