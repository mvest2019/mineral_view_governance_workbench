Question:
What is the source for the 300% penalty figure in the new diagram, and does it match the 100–200% / 300–400% ranges published elsewhere?

Answer:
The 300% in the diagram is the page's own explicitly-hedged worked example, and its source is the industry-standard AAPL Form 610 Model Form JOA non-consent provision — the most common Texas JOA structure, which typically charges the non-consenting party's share at roughly 100–200% on surface/equipment costs and 300–400% on drilling and completion costs; 300% is the conventional headline multiplier within that drilling/completion band. So yes, it matches: the two ranges describe different cost categories, and 300% sits inside the 300–400% drilling-cost range — there's no contradiction. The page itself carries the required hedge ("people often talk about a '300% penalty,' but don't assume every agreement uses 300%… the exact multiple comes from your executed agreement or pooling order, not an industry rule of thumb"), including the Texas exception that unleased common-law cotenants face no multiplier at all — so the diagram is an illustrative example of the common case, not a governed claim of a universal rate.
